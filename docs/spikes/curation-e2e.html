1. BOJ’s Ueda ‘can’t tolerate’ underlying inflation further over 2% — Bank of Japan Gov. Kazuo Ueda during the International Monetary Fund and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok on Thursday. | BLOOMBERG

Bank of Japan Gov. Kazuo Ueda said Friday that he “can’t tolerate” underlying inflation rising further above 2%, reinforcing expectations for policymakers to raise interest rates further in coming months.

“Underlying inflation is already roughly around 2%, and we cannot tolerate it any further going forward,” Ueda told reporters in Bangkok, where he was attending a gathering of global finance chiefs. He was responding to a question whether price gains nearing the BOJ’s target would have any impact on policy management.

The BOJ governor was speaking two weeks ahead of the bank’s next policy decision, with investors and economists currently anticipating no change at that gathering. Next week, Japan’s latest inflation report is expected to show overall prices rose 2.1% in September from a year before, with costs excluding fresh food also up 2.1% and prices subtracting both fresh food and energy 2.3% higher.

“While underlying inflation nears 2%, financial conditions remain accommodative,” Ueda said. “We will basically continue to raise the policy rate, depending on economic and price developments, and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation.”

He added that the bank will consider the timing and pace of hike while monitoring developments in the Middle East, AI-related demand and exchange rates, and assessing the risks surrounding them.

“The policy phase has recently shifted toward making sure inflation settles at around 2% as a key objective,” Ueda said after attending annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund and World Bank. Ueda said that his discussions with international counterparts hadn’t yielded much significant information that contradicted the BOJ’s current assessment.

Ueda said that he would discuss with fellow board members at the October meeting whether to tweak the bank’s current language describing underlying inflation as nearing its 2% target. On Thursday, BOJ board member Junko Koeda warned against too much caution over raising interest rates.

The yen was little changed after Ueda’s remarks, trading around ¥158 per dollar. The currency has remained above the four-decade low that it hit in July, before the U.S. participated with Japan in a rare joint intervention to support the yen. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who has been vocal about Japan’s monetary and economic policies since last year, has said the move was in the U.S. interest.

Bessent skipped the meetings in Bangkok, despite the U.S. holding the Group of 20 presidency. Ueda said he hasn’t communicated with Bessent since a G20 meeting in late August in the U.S.. He said he had exchanged views on a range of topics with Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh — who did attend the meetings in Bangkok — without offering specifics.

Overnight swaps have suggested that traders are pricing in less than 5% probability of a hike this month, with the chances rising to about 75% for a move by December.

Earlier this week, Bloomberg reported that BOJ officials are leaning against raising rates for a second consecutive meeting later this month, while keeping open the possibility of another move as soon as December, citing people familiar with the matter. Price developments have been broadly in line with the BOJ’s projections, with underlying inflation expected to reach a level consistent with its 2% target sometime in the next 18 months, the people said.

In September, the BOJ raised rates for the second time in three months, marking its fastest pace of monetary tightening since 1990. About a third of economists polled last month by Bloomberg forecast a move in December, with more than half predicting the BOJ would wait until January.
