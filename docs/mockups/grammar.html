MOCKUP · EXPLORATION · REVISED

Grammar in the reader

Revised to match the real reader: plain text with no furigana on the page (long-press still peeks the reading), the word panel at the bottom with EASY / MEDIUM / HARD, and 。 for the sentence. The new question is how a tap on a word differs from a tap on grammar. Tap anything in phone 1: phone 2 shows what opens. Tap a 。 to see that sentence in phone 3.

FIRST-MEET MARK

The tap problem

The reader already cuts every sentence into tap targets, and a grammar point doesn't line up with them. Here is how the app really cuts 「…住む前に直さなければなりません」, with 〜なければならない shaded:

住む前に直さなければなりません。
The point spans three taps, and it starts inside the word 直さなけれ. 住み始めています is worse: 住み | 始めて | います, with 〜始める and 〜ている overlapping inside 始めて.
RECOMMENDED

A · The tap decides

Keep one tap and one panel, and let what you tapped decide what leads. A word (直さなけれ, 住み) opens the word as now, with its grammar underneath it. A grammar piece (ば, なりません, ので, ながら, そう) opens the grammar point first. Every piece is already tappable, so there's nothing new to aim at. Phone 2.
RECOMMENDED · WITH A

B · The sentence lists it

Tap 。 as now: below the translation, the sheet lists every grammar point in the sentence. One place to see it all, and a sure way to reach a point whose pieces are small or awkward to hit. Phone 3.
NO

C · Separate grammar targets

The first mockup: grammar gets its own underlined tap target. It doesn't fit the real text. We'd have to re-cut words (直さなけれ → 直さ + なければ…) and two targets would compete in the same few millimetres.
MAYBE LATER

D · A 文法 lens

A switch in the reader header: while on, taps go to grammar and words dim. It's clean, but it's a mode you have to remember and you'd be flipping it all the time. Worth another look if A feels crowded.

How A + B work

  1. 1
    Every piece already opens something. Today a word tap opens the word and a particle tap looks the particle up in the dictionary. Grammar slots into the same tap. If the piece is a word, the panel is the word panel as now; its AI insight stays as now (relabelled INSIGHT, since it's about the word as much as the grammar), and below it a PATTERNS list shows the catalogue points that word takes part in (直す → 〜なければならない). If the piece is grammar (ば, なりません, ので, います, そう, です), the panel leads with the grammar point.
  2. 2
    The whole point lights up. When a grammar point is open, its full span gets a soft band in the text, across tokens (直さなければなりません). That way you see what the point covers even though you tapped one piece.
  3. 3
    Grammar and word, one step apart. Each grammar row in a word panel opens the point; ‹ goes back to the word. A grammar panel opened from a piece that is also a word (なりません is なる) has a quiet link to that word at the bottom.
  4. 4
    Same mastery as words, but looking isn't grading. EASY / MEDIUM / HARD, the same slider. Grammar rows shown in a word panel count for nothing. A grammar panel counts as a lookup only when opened from a row, or when it stays open for about 2 seconds after a tap on a grammar piece, so an accidental tap next to a word logs nothing. A lookup only makes the point a MEDIUM review candidate; only the slider sets EASY or HARD.
  5. 5
    Marks only on a first meeting. The page stays plain. Taps don't depend on marks, and the 。 sheet shows what a sentence holds. The one exception is a point you've never seen before: it gets a faint dotted underline the first time it appears, as a quiet “this is new”, and is never marked again. Most days that's zero to two marks per article. Toggle FIRST-MEET MARK off to see the fully plain page.
  6. 6
    AI for this sentence, catalogue for the pattern. The AI GRAMMAR INSIGHT keeps doing what it does well: explaining this word in this sentence of this article. The grammar panel gets one too, about the point in this sentence, and it leads. The catalogue supplies what should stay the same every time: the name, level, how it's built, and examples. When a sentence matches a catalogue point, the insight prompt is told the point, so the AI names the same pattern the rows show and doesn't contradict them. Words with no match (京都, 庭) get the insight alone, as now.
  7. 7
    Taller when there's grammar. The panel sizes to its content but stops at 52% of the screen today. When the word has PATTERNS rows, or a grammar point is open, the cap rises to about 70%, so the insight and the rows show without scrolling. A plain word (京都, 庭) opens at the usual height. The existing collision rule still applies: the panel opens from the far side and never covers the word you tapped, so near the top of the screen it gets whatever room is left.
