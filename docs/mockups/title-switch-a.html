Titles side by side, four ways

Four takes on A, built to compare how the switch moves and what it tells you. The toggles apply to all four phones: a hint on the other title saying something is waiting there, a third page on 読む (雑誌, for Phase C magazines), and the label language. Tap a small title or drag the page sideways. Phone 4 needs scrolling.

1 · FIXED ORDER, SIZE SWAP
The titles never move. The one you pick grows in place and the other shrinks, so News is always on the left and Library on the right, which helps muscle memory.
2 · ACTIVE FIRST
The current page is always in the title position at the left edge, like a normal heading. The row crossfades on a switch, so positions change.
3 · SCRUB WITH THE SWIPE
Drag the page sideways and the titles follow your finger: one grows as the other shrinks. Let go past halfway to switch, or before it to snap back.
4 · PINS SMALL ON SCROLL
Same as 1, but once the heading scrolls away a small bar of both titles pins to the top, so you can switch from deep in a list.

Ideas in the phones

  1. 1
    Fixed order vs. active first. Fixed order (1, 3, 4) keeps each page in one spot, and the size change alone says which page you're on. Active first (2) always puts the current page where a title normally sits, but positions jump on every switch. I lean toward fixed order.
  2. 2
    Hints on the other title. A count (Library ²: works in progress; News ³: articles ready; Podcasts ¹: unplayed) or just a dot. This gives a reason to switch without adding UI. The count is more informative; the dot is quieter and closer to the zen brief.
  3. 3
    Scrub. The title is a live indicator of the swipe, not just a result. It's the most physical option, but it has the same conflict with the Feed's card swipe as before, so the page drag would start from the heading or need more travel.
  4. 4
    Pin small on scroll. The News feed and Library get long. Without a pinned bar, switching means scrolling back up. The bar only appears after scrolling, so the resting page stays bare.
  5. 5
    A shorter word for podcasts. ポッドキャスト has no standard short form; clipped versions are only slang. 番組 ("show, programme") is a common everyday word, and 番組 | 音楽 pair neatly as two kanji each. Since it's a word readers will meet elsewhere, it's also worth learning. ラジオ is the casual alternative: many Japanese podcasters call their shows ラジオ. Set the label toggle to Japanese, then try each with the PODCAST WORD toggle.
  6. 5b
    Three pages. ニュース 本棚 雑誌 fits on one line in both languages (try the toggle). Music on 聞く makes two. Four is the practical limit.

Smaller ideas not built here

  1. 6
    Word cards for every title. Tapping the active title opens its word card, as now. A long-press on a small title could do the same without switching, so ニュース / 本棚 / 音楽 all stay learnable.
  2. 7
    The adaptive switch plays where the eye is. When a sub-page word is learned (News → ニュース), its English→Japanese switch animation should play even while that title is the small one, so it isn't missed.
  3. 8
    Remember the page per tab, and let 今日 deep-link to a page (すべて → ニュース, 続きを読む → 本棚). Tapping the nav icon of the tab you're already on could toggle pages, as a fast path.
  4. 9
    One component. A TitleSwitch replaces PageHeading's single title for 読む and 聞く (and possibly 復習 | 進捗). It takes the pages, the active one, per-page actions (+) and hints.
